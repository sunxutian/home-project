🚤 Tigre & Paraná Delta

Tigre is the classic river-delta town north of Buenos Aires, offering boat cruises through wooded island canals, boardwalks, and waterfront markets with zero car needed once you step off the train.

🚆 How to Get There (~1 hr 15 min total):

Take a 10-min Uber from Palermo Suites directly to Lisandro de la Torre or Belgrano C (no need to go downtown to Retiro!), then ride the Mitre Line (Ramal Tigre) north to San Isidro (~25 min) or Tigre (~45 min).

👶 What to Do With Kids (Ages 6 & 4):

Board a 1-hour wooden catamaran cruise through the Paraná Delta right next to the train station, then walk along the riverside promenade to browse Puerto de Frutos for handmade crafts, snacks, and river views.

💡 Tip for 6 People:

Reviewers note that Tigre Station can occasionally have issues recharging SUBE cards, so load your SUBE cards at a Palermo kiosk/Subte station before leaving (children under 3 ride free; ages 4 and 6 need standard SUBE taps, only ~$0.50–$1 USD each way).

⛪ San Isidro (Historic Suburb)

San Isidro is an upscale, leafy historic suburb along the same Mitre Line on the way to Tigre, making it easy to visit on its own or combine with Tigre on the same train line.

🚆 How to Get There (~50–60 min total):

Take the Mitre Line (Ramal Tigre) from Retiro (or connect via Belgrano C) to San Isidro station (~35 minutes on train), or take local Bus 60 from Palermo along Av. Cabildo.

👶 What to Do With Kids (Ages 6 & 4):

Walk up cobblestone streets to the soaring Neo-Gothic San Isidro Cathedral, let the kids run on the shaded lawns of Plaza Mitre, and browse the weekend artisan fair before stopping at a family-friendly café or riverside park.

💡 Tigre Combo Strategy:

Since San Isidro is halfway along the exact same Mitre train line to Tigre, you can stop off for 1.5 hours in the morning, tour the cathedral and park, and then hop back on the train for the final 20 minutes to Tigre!

Essential Argentina Must-Dos & Souvenir Guide

SUBE transit card, USD cash vs. credit cards, 21% VAT hotel exemption, power adapters & top gifts

Pre-Trip Checklist
🚇

SUBE Metro & Train Card

Buy 1–2 physical SUBE cards at a Palermo Kiosco or Subte station on Day 1 and load ~ARS 15,000–20,000 (~$15 USD). While newer Subte gates accept tap credit cards, Mitre trains (San Isidro/Tigre) and city buses still require SUBE. You can tap multiple family members on one card; under age 3 rides free, ages 4 & 6 pay standard ~$0.50–$1 taps.

💵

Cash, Credit Cards & Tipping

Foreign Visa/Mastercard cards work almost everywhere at the favorable tourist/MEP rate (always pay in ARS, never USD!). Still bring ~$200–$300 USD in crisp, unfolded, untorn $100 bills—exchange ~$100–$150 at a Cambio for ARS cash used for 10% restaurant tips, kioscos, street markets (San Telmo/Puerto de Frutos), and SUBE top-ups.

🧾

21% VAT (IVA) Hotel Exemption

Foreign tourists paying with a non-Argentine card and showing foreign passports at check-in are exempt from Argentina's 21% lodging VAT! Present all 6 passports at both Palermo Suites (Dec 5) and La Aldea de la Selva (#12324200, Dec 8) so you aren't charged extra IVA. For big Argentine-made leather/wool purchases, ask for Tax-Free (Global Blue) forms.

🛍️

Best Argentine Souvenirs to Buy

• Alfajores & Chocolates: Havanna (70% Cacao & classic boxes at Distrito Arcos/EZE), Cachafaz, Rapanui.
• Wine: Mendoza Malbec (Catena, Luigi Bosca, Zuccardi) at local vinotecas (~1/3 US price).
• Traditional: Carved Mate gourd + metal bombilla straw + Playadito yerba; Argentine leather belts/bags.
• For Kids: Messi #10 jersey, Mafalda figurines (San Telmo), & Guaraní wood animal carvings (Iguazú).

🔌

Apps, Passports & Power Plugs

• Rideshare: Install & verify Uber and Cabify before flying from JFK (always keep both handy for 2 cars).
• Passports: Carry physical passports on domestic Iguazú flights (AEP ⇄ IGR) and hotel check-ins.
• Power: Argentina uses 220V with Type I (3 flat angled prongs) and some Type C (2 round pins) — pack universal adapters!

Local Rhythm & Kid Tips

• Dinner Timing: Locals eat at 9:00 PM+, so arriving at 7:00–7:30 PM gets you instant tables with kids!
• Water: Tap water in Buenos Aires is safe, though bottled water (sin gas) is best for sensitive stomachs.
• Safety: Palermo & Recoleta are very walkable; in La Boca stay on Caminito & keep backpacks worn in front in crowds.
